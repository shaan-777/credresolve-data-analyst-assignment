# Executive Memo: Collections Recovery Performance and ₹10 Cr Investment Recommendation

## To: Collections Leadership  
## Subject: Reconstruction of Actual Recovery, Validation of the Reported 11% Improvement, and ₹10 Cr Investment Recommendation

---

## Executive Summary

The collections data was analysed to reconstruct actual recovery performance, evaluate campaign attribution, validate the reported 11% improvement, and recommend deployment of the proposed ₹10 Cr investment.

The analysis covered **23,344 targeted accounts** and identified **₹1.879 billion (approximately ₹187.89 Cr) of actual recovery** based on the reconstructed payment data.

Campaign-level attribution, however, reported **₹2.791 billion (approximately ₹279.11 Cr)** of recovery. This is materially higher than actual recovery and produces a recovery inflation ratio of **1.49x**. The difference is primarily explained by overlapping campaign assignments and attribution of the same payment across multiple campaigns.

The reported **11% improvement could not be validated**. The measured improvement from the analysis was only **2.99%**, which is approximately **8.01 percentage points below the reported claim**.

Therefore, the recommended approach is to invest the ₹10 Cr selectively in the highest-performing campaigns while correcting attribution methodology and data-quality issues before using campaign-attributed recovery as a primary investment metric.

---

# 1. What Actually Happened?

A total of **23,344 accounts** were targeted through the campaign programme.

After reconstructing recovery using payment-level data, the analysis found:

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Total accounts targeted | 23,344 |
| Total actual recovery | ₹187.89 Cr |
| Campaign-attributed recovery | ₹279.11 Cr |
| Recovery inflation ratio | 1.49x |

The campaign-attributed recovery is approximately **48.5% higher than the reconstructed actual recovery**.

This means campaign reporting should not be interpreted as incremental cash generated by campaigns without applying a deterministic attribution methodology.

The main finding is therefore:

> **Actual recovery performance is materially lower than campaign-attributed recovery reporting suggests.**

---

# 2. Validation of the Reported 11% Improvement

The reported campaign improvement was tested by comparing recovery in the 30-day period before and after campaign targeting.

The results were:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| Total recovery — 30 days before | ₹35.53 Cr |
| Total recovery — 30 days after | ₹36.59 Cr |
| Absolute recovery uplift | ₹1.06 Cr |
| Measured improvement | **2.99%** |
| Reported improvement | **11.00%** |
| Difference from reported claim | **-8.01 percentage points** |
| Accounts analysed | 6,579 |

The measured improvement of **2.99%** is substantially below the reported **11%**.

Therefore:

> **The available data does not support the claim that campaigns produced an 11% recovery improvement.**

The observed uplift may also include effects unrelated to campaign activity, such as natural repayment behaviour, repeated targeting, overlapping campaigns, timing effects, or pre-existing customer payment propensity.

# 4. Data Quality and Attribution Risks

The analysis identified several material risks.

## A. Accounts assigned to multiple campaigns — HIGH RISK

**13,235 accounts**, representing **56.70% of targeted accounts**, were assigned to more than one campaign.

This is the most significant attribution problem.

If the same account receives multiple campaigns and subsequently makes a payment, that payment can potentially be attributed to multiple campaigns.

This can result in:

- Double counting of recovery
- Inflated campaign performance
- Incorrect campaign rankings
- Incorrect investment allocation

---

## B. Repeated targeting within the same campaign — MEDIUM RISK

There were **282 repeated targeting relationships**, representing approximately **0.63%** of targeting relationships.

Repeated exposure makes a simple before-versus-after comparison less reliable because the observed outcome cannot necessarily be linked to a single campaign interaction.

---

## C. Campaign-attributed payment overlap — HIGH RISK

The analysis identified approximately **₹9.12 Cr of campaign-attributed payment overlap**, equivalent to approximately **48.55% of actual recovery**.

This confirms that campaign-level recovery attribution materially exceeds the unique underlying recovery that actually occurred.

Therefore:

> **Campaign-attributed recovery should not be summed across campaigns and treated as total cash recovery.**

---

# 5. ₹10 Cr Investment Recommendation

The ₹10 Cr investment should **not** be distributed equally across all campaigns.

Instead, investment should be concentrated on campaigns demonstrating strong recovery and conversion performance.

The recommended investment framework is:

### Tier 1 — Scale proven high performers
Prioritize campaigns with:

- High total recovery
- Conversion rates around or above 60%
- Adequate account volume
- Strong relative ranking in the campaign performance analysis

This includes campaigns such as:

- CMP000092
- CMP000082
- CMP000007
- CMP000023
- CMP000024

### Tier 2 — Controlled expansion
Allocate controlled investment to additional high-performing campaigns while monitoring incremental performance.

### Tier 3 — Do not scale until measurement improves
Avoid aggressively scaling campaigns where apparent performance may be driven by overlapping attribution rather than incremental recovery.

---

# 6. Recommended Measurement Framework Going Forward

Before scaling campaign investment further, recovery attribution should be changed to a deterministic framework.

A payment should be attributed to **only one campaign** using a predefined rule, for example:

1. Identify valid successful campaign contact events.
2. Match each payment to the relevant customer account.
3. Attribute the payment to the most recent eligible campaign contact before payment.
4. Apply a fixed attribution window.
5. Ensure each payment is counted once only.

Future campaign evaluation should also include a control or comparison group where possible.

The primary performance metrics should be:

- Unique actual recovery
- Incremental recovery versus baseline/control
- Conversion rate
- Recovery per targeted account
- Recovery per ₹ invested
- Incremental ROI

---

# Final Recommendation

The campaign programme is associated with positive recovery activity, but the currently reported **11% improvement is not supported by the reconstructed analysis**.

The measured improvement is **2.99%**, while campaign-attributed recovery is inflated to **1.49x actual recovery** because of significant overlap and multi-campaign assignment.

The ₹10 Cr investment should therefore be deployed selectively toward the strongest campaigns, while attribution logic is corrected before further large-scale investment decisions are made.

## Management Decision

**Recommended action:**

> Proceed with selective investment in the highest-performing campaigns, but do not use aggregated campaign-attributed recovery as evidence of incremental performance until payment attribution is made mutually exclusive and deterministic.

This approach balances continued recovery growth with materially improved measurement reliability and reduces the risk of allocating capital based on double-counted recovery.
